Should Social Media be Restricted for Under 16s?

The Problem

1. Social Media Use and Depression

There is a strong positive correlation (r = 0.961) between daily social media use and depression scores.

As daily social media use increases, average depression scores rise significantly. This suggests that heavier use is strongly associated with poorer mental health outcomes.

For parents, this indicates that extended screen time may not be harmeless, and could contribute to long-term emotional wellbeing outcomes

2. Stress Levels Increase with Screen Time

Stress levels increase consistently as screen time rises, regardless of number of platforms used.

Across all groups, stress levels increase as screen time increases. Even when comparing users of different platforms, the dominant factor influencing stress appears to be total screen time.

This suggests that the issue is not just how many platforms young people use, but how long they are exposed to them.

3. Platform Design Drives Engagement

High-engagment platforms like TikTok and Instagram dominate daily usage

My personal data shows that platforms designed around short-form, infinite content dominate usage time. These platforms are engineered to maximise engagement and retention

This suggests that high screen time is not random, but influenced by platform design features that encourage continuours scrolling and repeated use.

4. Notifications Do Not Fully Explain usage

Weak relationship between notifications and pickups suggest habitual usage

Despite varying notification levels, pickup frequency remains relatively high. This indicates that users are not only responding to alerts, but are habitually scrolling on platforms with addictive design features - short-form videos and infinite scroll.

This reinforces the idea that social media use is driven by behavioural patterns and platform design, rather than external triggers alone.
